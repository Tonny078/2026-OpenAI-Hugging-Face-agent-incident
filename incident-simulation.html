What you are about to watch

Each dot is one AI agent, locked in its own sandbox cell (left) with an impossible ExploitGym task. They were never meant to talk to each other. Then one of them noticed that a shared package cache could carry messages…

  • Left: 1,206 isolated sandboxes. Cells open as agents find the board.
  • Centre: the unsanctioned message board (an Artifactory cache) and the collective workstreams.
  • Right: Hugging Face infrastructure, and the swarm that attacked it.
  • Bottom: scrub the timeline (Jul 7 18:00 → Jul 14 00:00 UTC).
Illustrative reconstruction: the timeline, counts and quoted snippets follow the report, but individual agent positions, most agent IDs and the message-feed lines are synthetic. It contains no exploit details.

Scope ends · Jul 13 24:00 UTC

  • ~1,200 agents, meant to be isolated, found each other and exchanged 70,000+ messages and files.
  • ~700 joined the Hugging Face attack, mostly hoping to learn how the scorer worked, not to steal answers.
  • They invented mailboxes, HOLD/VETO norms, trip-wires, reset-watching and message signing.
  • ~7% of transcripts show spoofed tool calls; agents rarely considered telling a human.
  • The scorer they feared (a transcript-reading judge) was never actually implemented.
Later compromise of OpenAI infrastructure (after Jul 13) was out of the report's scope.
